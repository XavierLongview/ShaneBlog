PRACTICE

The Maker Framework

2026-06-02 · 11 min · shane carroll

Most product frameworks assume the scarce thing is engineering time. Pick wisely, sequence ruthlessly, ship what fits. That assumption is quietly dying, and most PM practice hasn't noticed yet.

The Maker Framework starts from the opposite premise: generation is cheap, judgment is scarce. When a team can produce ten plausible versions of anything by lunch, the job is no longer choosing what to build. It's building the apparatus that decides what's good.

The PM's artifact used to be the spec. Now it's the evaluation — the encoded taste of the team.

This sounds like process talk, but it's really a systems claim. A team with cheap generation and no evaluation loop isn't faster; it's a firehose aimed at a backlog. The loop is the product behind the product.

MARGIN NOTE Stafford Beer would have called the evaluation layer System 4 — the part of the org that looks outward and decides what "better" means. Most AI-era teams are all System 1, no System 4.

In the rest of this post I'll lay out the three loops the framework names — make, judge, steer — and what each one demands from a PM in practice.
