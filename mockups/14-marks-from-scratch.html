A five-pointed star is secretly a circuit: it can be drawn in one continuous closed stroke. So draw it that way — leave the stroke visibly unclosed and put an arrowhead on it. From across the room it's a red star; up close it's a feedback loop that never stops being traced. The politics and the cybernetics are literally the same shape, which is roughly your whole thesis. Gold dot at the center: the commons the loop encloses.

A feedback loop never returns to the same place — each pass through changes the system. That's not a circle; that's a spiral. This one carries the rest of the brief for free: it's self-similar at every scale (recursion), it narrows from wide chaos to a focused center (attenuation), and it's a galaxy with a red star at the heart of it (you know what that is). The arrow marks the direction: inward, toward sense.

The attenuator idea, completed instead of repaired. Beer's variety engineering was never just compression — it's a bowtie: the world's variety attenuated into the regulator, and the regulator's response amplified back out (green in, gold out). The single red stroke beneath is the world itself closing the loop — what you do comes back as what you face next. The most technically literate mark on the sheet, and symmetric enough to sit anywhere.

A plant whose two branches don't end in air — they arc all the way back down and feed their own roots, arrows pointing home. Growth as a closed loop: what the system produces returns to sustain it. One fruit gold (what you made), one left paper-blank (what the reader brings), a red bud at the crown where the branches split. The warmest and most organic of the four — the one that says the future is a garden, not a server room.
